How it started

Arpit Pandey first encountered the handpan at a meditation retreat in Rishikesh in 2016. He had gone there looking for stillness. What he found instead was sound — specifically, a single note struck on a steel dome that seemed to hold the whole room in suspension.

He spent the next year searching for an instrument of his own, studying the handful of makers in India and eventually acquiring a Pantam in D Kurd. What began as a personal practice slowly became something larger.

"I didn't choose the handpan. It felt more like it chose me — at exactly the moment I needed something that could hold both music and silence at the same time."

The music

Arpit's compositions move between the composed and the improvised. His debut album Mitti aur Sur (Earth and Melody) draws on Indian classical sensibilities while embracing the meditative, cyclical quality that makes handpan music so distinctive globally.

He has performed at meditation retreats, yoga festivals, corporate mindfulness events, and intimate living room concerts across Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi, and Rishikesh. Each performance is shaped by the space and the people in it.

Teaching philosophy

Arpit believes that the handpan is not primarily a technical instrument — it is an instrument of listening. His workshops and classes are designed around that idea: slow down, listen deeply, and let the music emerge from genuine feeling rather than mechanical repetition.

He has taught over 200 students across India and internationally through online classes, ranging from complete beginners who have never played an instrument before to experienced musicians discovering the handpan for the first time.
