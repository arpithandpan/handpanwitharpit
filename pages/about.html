How it started

Where the handpan found him

Arpit grew up in Uttar Pradesh and was drawn to rhythm long before he found the handpan.

In 2015, a YouTube video stopped him mid-scroll: David Kuckhermann playing alongside Naghmeh Farahmand on tonbak, two instruments in conversation, the room completely still. That was his first encounter with the handpan. He got one shortly after and hasn’t put it down since.

Most of the early learning happened outdoors, wherever there was a quiet corner to sit.

The roots

A direction, a standard

Arpit had the rare privilege of meeting the legendary tabla maestro Pt. Lachhu Maharaj, briefly, just ten days before he passed. That meeting never became lessons, but it became something else: a direction, and a standard. Pt. Lachhu Maharaj remains his guru.

The music

From living rooms to open-air festivals

Now based in Bengaluru, Arpit performs, records and composes music that sits somewhere between the meditative and the melodic. Each performance is shaped by the space and the people in it.

Teaching

Slow down, listen deeply

Since 2022, Arpit has taught through live workshops in Bengaluru and online classes on Zoom, with students joining from India and abroad.

His approach is simple: slow down, listen deeply, and let the playing come from feeling rather than repetition. Every class is built around where the student is, not a fixed syllabus.
