How it started

Arpit grew up in Uttar Pradesh with rhythm in his bones, drawn to sound long before he found the instrument that would define him.

In 2015, a YouTube video stopped him mid-scroll. David Kuckhermann playing alongside Naghmeh Farahmand on tonbak, two instruments in conversation, the room completely still. That was his first encounter with the handpan. He got one shortly after and hasn't put it down since.

The roots

His musical roots go deeper. He had the rare privilege of meeting the legendary tabla maestro Pt. Lachhu Maharaj, briefly just ten days before he passed. That meeting never became lessons, but it became something else. A direction. A standard. Pt. Lachhu Maharaj remains his guru.

The music

Now based in Bengaluru, Arpit performs, records, and composes, releasing music that moves between the meditative and the melodic. From intimate living room concerts to open-air festivals, each performance is shaped by the space and the people in it.

Teaching

Since 2022, Arpit has been teaching handpan through live workshops in Bengaluru and online classes on Zoom. His approach is simple: slow down, listen deeply, and let the music emerge from genuine feeling rather than mechanical repetition.
